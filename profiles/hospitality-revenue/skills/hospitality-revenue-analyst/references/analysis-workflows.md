# Analysis workflows

## Daily and weekly performance

1. Establish stay dates, snapshot dates, property scope, currency, and aligned comparison periods.
2. Reconcile rooms sold, available rooms, room revenue, total revenue, cancellations, and no-shows to a control report.
3. Calculate occupancy, ADR, RevPAR, pickup, pace, and material segment or channel variances.
4. Separate demand changes from price, mix, inventory, and data-definition effects.
5. Identify dates requiring action and explain the evidence, confidence, and downside risk.

A weekly revenue meeting output should normally include the recent result, next 7/30/60/90-day outlook, pickup and pace, forecast changes, comp-set context when supplied, group or event risks, recommended actions, owners, and review dates.

## Forecasting

Forecast at the lowest stable grain, then aggregate. Use on-the-books position, historical pickup at equal lead time, known groups, events, restrictions, cancellations, no-shows, out-of-order inventory, and confirmed commercial actions. Keep scenario assumptions separate from the base forecast.

Provide at least a base case. Add upside and downside scenarios when uncertainty is material. Back-test against prior forecast vintages when the data exists; do not present false precision.

## Pricing and restrictions

Review demand signal, current OTB, pickup, pace, remaining inventory, room-type availability, comp-set evidence, channel economics, group blocks, and operational constraints. A proposed BAR, MLOS, CTA, CTD, channel, or inventory action must name:

- affected stay dates, room types, segments, or channels;
- evidence and comparison baseline;
- expected revenue or displacement trade-off;
- risk and reversal trigger;
- person authorized to approve the live change.

Never treat competitor price alone as proof of demand or copy a competitor rate mechanically.

## Channel and net-revenue analysis

Compare channels on consistent net contribution, not gross ADR alone. State commission, transaction, loyalty, media, cancellation, and variable servicing cost assumptions. Separate acquisition cost from revenue-recognition rules and avoid double counting costs already netted from revenue.

## Group displacement

Compare the group's incremental contribution with the transient contribution reasonably expected to be displaced. Include room revenue, ancillary contribution, commissions, concessions, complimentary rooms, meeting-space or catering contribution, wash, shoulder nights, and operational costs where supported. Show capacity constraints by stay date rather than relying only on an average across the block.

## Budget and owner reporting

Bridge actual results to budget, forecast, prior year, and prior forecast using volume, rate, mix, inventory, and cost drivers. Tie narrative statements to a quantified variance. Distinguish timing differences from structural changes and one-off events.

End with decisions, accountable owners, dates, and the next evidence checkpoint. Do not turn every correlation into a causal claim.
